Careers and where to go next

You now have the basics. Security work is wide: the first video groups it into eight areas, from managing risk to running a security operations centre to advising software teams. People arrive from many backgrounds, and the UK has official ways to explore: the NCSC's cyber skills programme, which sits within the government's wider TechFirst programme, NCSC-certified degrees and assured training, and National Careers Service job profiles.

Where next? If the idea of designing security in from the start appealed, thinking about how a whole service fits together and what could go wrong, the next stage is our beginner security architecture path. It assumes only what you have learned here, and builds to a first exercise using real templates.

Try it for yourself

  • Pick one of the eight areas that sounds interesting. Find a National Careers Service job profile that fits it, and note one skill you would like to practise.

Videos to watch

These are clips from longer free videos. Each link starts at the right moment, and you are welcome to watch only that part.

  • YouTube · starts at 36:40 · about 8 minutes · part of a video that runs 1 hour 14 minutes

    Cybersecurity for Beginners: Eight security domains (opens in a new tab)

    A map of the kinds of work in security, grouped into eight areas defined by a professional certification body. Handy for spotting which area you might enjoy.

    What this video covers

    • Professionals group their work into eight areas: security and risk management, asset security, security architecture and engineering, communication and network security, identity and access management, assessment and testing, security operations, and software development security.
    • Each comes with an everyday example: updating policies when a law changes, safely disposing of old equipment, setting up a firewall, writing a network policy, issuing keycards, auditing who can see payroll, responding when an unknown device appears, and advising app teams on password rules.
    • The areas are linked, and a gap in one can harm the whole organisation.
    • You do not need to master them all. Noticing which one appeals to you can help steer what you learn next. Security architecture and engineering is the area our beginner architecture path picks up.

  • YouTube · starts at 55:23 · about 10 minutes · part of a video that runs 1 hour 30 minutes

    How To Prepare For Your Cybersecurity Career: Find and prepare for a job in cybersecurity (opens in a new tab)

    Three entry-level roles and how to start looking. It is written for the US job market, so the job sites and statistics it names are American; the UK links below are the ones to use.

    What this video covers

    • A security analyst usually monitors networks for breaches, helps plan how to secure an organisation and keeps up with new threats.
    • An information security analyst focuses on creating plans and putting protections in place. A security operations centre analyst makes sure incidents are handled quickly and consistently, following agreed procedures.
    • Research the organisation and the role before you apply, and use job sites to see which skills employers ask for.
    • No security experience is fine. List what you have learned, and the skills you already have that carry over, such as attention to detail, teamwork and clear communication.
