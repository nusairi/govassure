What cyber security is

Cyber security is the practice of protecting the devices, accounts, networks and information we all rely on, from people who should not have access to them and from honest accidents too. The UK's National Cyber Security Centre (NCSC) describes its core job as helping people and organisations reduce the risk and impact of cyber attacks.

You do not need to be technical to start. Nearly everything in security comes back to four questions: what are we protecting, who might want to get at it, how could they, and what is the simplest thing that would stop them? The rest of this path builds those questions up one step at a time.

Three ideas turn up everywhere: Confidentiality, Integrity and Availability.

  • Confidentiality means only the right people can see something. Only you can see your bank balance.
  • Integrity means it is correct and has not been quietly changed. Your balance shows what you really have.
  • Availability means the right people can get to it when they need it. You can log in when you need to pay a bill.

People often call these three “the CIA triad”. If you remember nothing else from this step, remember those three words.

Try it for yourself

  • Think of one account you use every day, such as email. Who should be able to see it? How would you notice if something had been changed? What would happen if you could not get in for a week? Those are the three ideas, applied to your own life.

Videos to watch

These are clips from longer free videos. Each link starts at the right moment, and you are welcome to watch only that part.

  • YouTube · starts at 10:16 · about 7 minutes · part of a video that runs 1 hour 14 minutes

    Cybersecurity for Beginners: What is cybersecurity? (opens in a new tab)

    A plain definition of security, what a security team does for an organisation, and what an entry-level analyst does day to day. It is made for a US audience, so the job-market figures near the end are American; use the UK links below for your own plans.

    What this video covers

    • Security means protecting networks, devices, people and data from being accessed or misused by people who should not have access, so that information stays private, correct and available.
    • A threat actor is any person or group that poses a risk. Threats can come from outside an organisation, or from inside, and inside threats are often accidents, such as someone clicking a bad link.
    • A security team helps an organisation have fewer breaches, follow laws and standards, keep working after an incident, and keep the trust of the people it serves.
    • An entry-level analyst watches the network and is usually first to respond when something is spotted, looks for weak points in the organisation's own systems, helps stop problems before they start, and checks records in security reviews.
    • These jobs exist in small businesses, charities, large companies and government alike.

  • YouTube · starts at 35:39 · about 4 minutes · part of a video that runs 1 hour 27 minutes

    How To Manage Security Risks & Threats: The CIA triad (opens in a new tab)

    A short, clear tour of the three ideas people use to think about any system, using a bank as the example.

    What this video covers

    • Confidentiality means only authorised people can see particular information, on a need-to-know basis.
    • Integrity means the information is correct, genuine and can be relied on.
    • Availability means the right people can reach the information when they need it. Locked-away data is no use to anyone.
    • A bank shows all three at once: it keeps account details private, it spots unusual spending to check the real owner is behind it, and it keeps online banking reachable.
    • Takeaway: when you hear about any security problem, ask which of the three it hurts.

What GOV.UK recommends

These official pages explain what cyber security is and where an ordinary person should start.

  • NCSC: What is cyber security? (opens in a new tab)

    The NCSC's plain explanation of what cyber security is for, and why it matters to people, to organisations and to the services the country relies on.

  • NCSC: Top tips for staying secure online (opens in a new tab)

    Short, practical actions anyone can take: a strong and separate email password, 2-step verification, software updates, backups and password managers. This is where the old Cyber Aware web address now leads.

  • NCSC: Cyber security advice for you and your family (opens in a new tab)

    A starting point for people at home, gathering the NCSC's advice on protecting your devices and accounts and on what to do if something goes wrong.

Check your understanding. A friend says: "Nobody would bother attacking me. I have nothing worth stealing." Using what you have just learned, how could you reply?

Show a good answer

Most attacks are not aimed at one person. They are sent to huge numbers of people in the hope that some will fall for them. Your email account, photos, contacts and identity are all valuable to a criminal, and a hacked account can be used to trick the people you know. Confidentiality, integrity and availability are exactly what you would lose.
