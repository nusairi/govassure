Human Impacts Cyber Framework · v1.0

The Personal Cyber Attack Taxonomy

A reference classification of cyber attacks that target people rather than enterprises. Forty attack types are organised into six threat domains and mapped across four dimensions — the attacker's goal, the delivery vector, the target asset, and the primary harm to the victim.

Foundational source: The Cyber Helpline — Recovery Guides. Each attack type links to its corresponding victim-support guide.

D1

Ransom, Ransomware & Malware

Malicious software and coercion used to deny access to data or devices, or to extract payment through threats.

D1.01

Encrypting Ransomware

Malware encrypts the victim's files and changes their extensions; a ransom is demanded for the decryption key while the device otherwise still works.

goal Extortion vector Malware harm Financial loss
D1.02

Screen-Locking Ransomware

A ransom note appears the moment the device is switched on, locking the victim out of the device entirely until payment.

goal Extortion vector Malware harm Financial loss
D1.03

Content for Ransom

An attacker obtains sensitive information about the victim and demands payment or action to prevent its release.

goal Extortion vector Coercion harm Financial loss
D1.04

Sextortion

Threats to share intimate images or video of the victim unless money is paid or demands are met.

goal Extortion vector Coercion harm Psychological & financial
D1.05

Malware Infection

Software is placed on a device to steal information or cause damage, without the owner's knowledge.

goal Data theft / damage vector Malware harm Data loss

Harassment, Stalking & Surveillance

The internet and connected devices used to bully, intimidate, monitor, impersonate or humiliate a person.

D3.01

Online Harassment

Bullying or abuse online, including trolling and abusive direct messages.

goal Psychological harm vector Online abuse harm Psychological
D3.02

Cyberstalking

Persistent online stalking accompanied by severe threats to the victim's personal safety.

goal Intimidation vector Online abuse harm Physical & psychological
D3.03

Outing / Doxxing

Sharing someone's personal information, secrets or sensitive images online without consent.

goal Reputation damage vector Data exposure harm Privacy & psychological
D3.04

Fake Profiles / Impersonation

Fake profiles created to harass the victim, or impersonating the victim to cause distress.

goal Impersonation vector Identity abuse harm Reputational
D3.05

Fraping

D3.06

Someone pretends to be a different person to trick the victim into a personal relationship.

goal Harm / radicalisation vector Content exposure harm Psychological

An ex-partner shares sexually explicit images or video of the victim without consent to cause distress.

goal Reputation damage vector Data exposure harm Psychological & reputational

D4

Social Engineering

Direct deception by email, phone or text that manipulates the victim into compromising their own security.

D4.01

Phishing

A deceptive email that tricks the victim into clicking, downloading or disclosing credentials.

goal Credential / data theft vector Deception (email) harm Data & financial
D4.02

Vishing

A fraudulent phone call that manipulates the victim into disclosing information or taking harmful action.

goal Credential / data theft vector Deception (voice) harm Financial loss
D4.03

Smishing

A fraudulent text message that tricks the victim into clicking a link or disclosing information.

goal Credential / data theft vector Deception (SMS) harm Data & financial

Deception intended to produce financial or personal gain for the criminal at the victim's expense.

D5.01

Virtual Currency Scams

The victim is defrauded through a cryptocurrency scheme or fake exchange.

goal Financial gain vector Deception harm Financial loss
D5.02

Card Fraud

Unauthorised transactions made using the victim's bank card details.

goal Financial gain vector Identity abuse harm Financial loss
D5.03

Identity Theft

An attacker steals and uses the victim's identity, often to open accounts or obtain credit.

goal Identity abuse vector Identity abuse harm Financial & reputational
D5.04

Online Auction Fraud

The victim is deceived when buying on an auction or marketplace site such as eBay or Amazon.

goal Financial gain vector Deception harm Financial loss
D5.05

Event-Based Scams

Scams that exploit a topical event or crisis (e.g. Covid-19) to add urgency and credibility.

goal Financial gain vector Deception harm Financial loss
D5.06

Recruitment Scams

Job-fraud schemes that deceive the victim while applying for work at home or abroad.

goal Financial gain vector Deception harm Financial loss
D5.07

Loan Scams

Fraud committed against the victim while they are taking out a loan.

goal Financial gain vector Deception harm Financial loss
D5.08

Investment Scams

The victim is defrauded while making what they believe to be a legitimate investment.

goal Financial gain vector Deception harm Financial loss

goal N/A (accidental) vector Human error harm Privacy & data loss

goal Reputation damage vector Data exposure harm Reputational

A company, app or service the victim uses is hacked, exposing the victim's data (third-party breach).

goal Data theft vector Third-party breach harm Privacy & data loss

How to read a classification code. Codes follow D#.## — the domain number, then the attack index within it (e.g. D4.02 = Social Engineering › Vishing).

This taxonomy is a descriptive reference model. It does not rank severity or likelihood; a single incident may span several types (for example, a phishing email that leads to account compromise and then fraud).
