Beginner path

Your first exercise, and where to go next

You have enough now to do a first piece of real work, using templates that already live in this Corner. There is no extra video for this step. Use what you have just learned, and keep the answers short. A first pass does not have to be complete.

  1. Open the data and sensitivity summary. For a service you know — even a made-up booking form is fine — write down what data it holds, why, and how sensitive it is.
  2. Open the threat model. Add two or three ways the service could go wrong, using the habits from earlier steps: someone signing in who should not, a device you do not trust, data going somewhere it should not, or a problem nobody notices.
  3. If one of those risks feels serious, add it to the risk register in one or two sentences, and write what you would do first.

That is a complete first pass. When you are ready, go back to the lifecycle map and follow the work for the phase you are actually in. The other templates and the pattern library are there when you need them.

What GOV.UK recommends

Secure by Design expects a first pass at these same artefacts — knowing what you hold, how important it is, and how it could go wrong — rather than a late formal review.

  • Secure by Design: Documenting service assets (opens in a new tab)

    Teams are expected to write down what the service is made of and what data it holds, so later security work has a clear starting point.

  • Secure by Design: Assessing the importance of service assets (opens in a new tab)

    Once you know what you hold, you need a view of how important it is if it is lost, changed or seen by the wrong person. That is what a first data and sensitivity summary is for.

  • Secure by Design: Performing threat modelling (opens in a new tab)

    The same activity as in step 5: write down how the service could go wrong and what you would do about it. Your first threat model is the start of that work, not the finished version.
