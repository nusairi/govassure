Networks and data

The other two building blocks you will hear about constantly are networks and data.

A network is how users, staff and other systems reach the service. A common approach is to divide it on purpose, so that a problem in one place cannot immediately reach everything else, and to allow only the traffic you mean to allow. The same idea applies when a service runs in the cloud. This series does not have a separate cloud video; when you are ready, use the Corner's Secure cloud landing zone pattern, and the official cloud principles below.

Data is often the reason the service exists, and the thing you most need to protect. Start by writing down what you hold, why you hold it, and how sensitive it is. Then you can talk about protection, such as limiting who can see it and encrypting it when that is needed, and about how you would recover if it was lost.

You do not need to know this yet: the networks video is long and names a lot of products and patterns. Skip the product names. Keep the picture of dividing the network and only allowing the traffic you intend.

What GOV.UK recommends

Official guidance covers the same two building blocks — how the network is designed, and how data is protected — including when the service runs in the cloud.

  • NCSC network security fundamentals (opens in a new tab)

    A plain introduction to designing a secure network: know what is on it, divide it up, restrict access, and protect data as it travels.

  • NCSC cloud security principles (opens in a new tab)

    What good looks like when a service runs in the cloud, including who can access it and how data is protected. Use this when you do not have a separate cloud video.

  • NCSC 10 Steps: Data security (opens in a new tab)

    Official advice on protecting data throughout its life: knowing what you hold, limiting who can see it, and being able to recover it.

Check your understanding. A team can describe every server in their design, but nobody can say what personal information the service will hold or where it will be stored. Which building block still needs work, and what would you do first?
